📘 Applying STEM Pedagogy

From theory → classroom practice → student learning

👥 Think–Pair–Share

How it works:

1. Think individually about a problem or question

2. Pair with a partner or small group

3. Share and compare reasoning with the class

In the classroom:

- Encourages participation from all students

- Gives quieter students structured speaking opportunities

- Helps students compare reasoning strategies

Example (Calculus):

- Solve a derivative problem individually

- Compare steps and methods with a partner

- Discuss why different approaches still reach the same result

Why it matters: Builds communication, reasoning, and confidence in problem-solving.

⏳ Wait Time

How it works:

- Pause after asking a question before calling on students

- Allow silence for thinking time

In the classroom:

- Longer pauses led to deeper and more complete answers

- Students were more willing to explain reasoning

- Reduced rushed or surface-level responses

Example:

- “Why does this limit approach infinity?”

- Wait 5–10 seconds before responding or prompting further

Why it matters: Supports deeper cognitive processing and equity in participation.

🌱 Growth Mindset

How it works:

- Focus on progress, effort, and improvement

- Normalize mistakes as part of learning

In the classroom:

- Students are encouraged to revise thinking

- Feedback emphasizes process, not just correctness

- Mistakes are treated as learning opportunities

Example:

- “Your setup is improving—try refining this step”

- “What did you learn from this attempt?”

Why it matters: Builds resilience and long-term confidence in STEM learning.
